The four jobs

Jobs that no arm can finish alone.

Each job comes in three team sizes and three levels, 36 tasks in all. A bigger team has more arms and more work to divide; a higher level makes the same job physically or visually harder. Each clip is a real run at 300 times real time, and opens it.

Transport

Deliver every item to its place on the right board. Any arm may carry any item, and items cross the table by being handed over or set down for the next arm.

Arms
4, 8, 12
Levels
Flat targets, then sockets, then sockets on a sprung board

Mosaic

Build a reference picture, one colored cube per cell. The picture has four colors, and every cell needs the right one.

Arms
4, 6, 8
Levels
Pockets, then printed lines, then unmarked cells

Relay

Carry long timbers to the goal. It takes at least two arms to carry one, and each timber must change hands in the air at least once.

Arms

Levels
Straight, then curved with a hanging weight, then hinged with a hanging weight

Dial

Find a hidden code by turning spring-loaded knobs and holding them all at once. The only hint is a display of the best try so far.

Arms

Levels
Knobs on the table, then modules held in the air, then cranks

What is new

Many arms, long jobs, and a world that never waits.

Earlier benchmarks give an agent one or two arms for minutes, often pause the world while it thinks, or tell it where every object is. RoboTeam takes all three away.

  • Up to 16 arms, up to 24 hoursTeams of 4 to 16 arms on one job, and runs that last hours instead of minutes.
  • The world never pausesThe hall keeps moving while the agents think, as a real factory would.
  • Cameras onlyNo object positions, no depth: the agents must measure the world from pictures.

How a run works

Inside one real run.

Agents work in a loop: look through the cameras, decide, send one command to one arm, and look again. Here is that loop in a run of gpt-6.1-sol on a Transport job with 8 arms, which it finished, all 24 items in place.

  1. 1

    See

    Only pictures

    Each arm has cameras. No positions come with the pictures: the agent has to work them out.

  2. 2

    Decide

    Split the work

    Ten minutes in, the controller starts a helper and hands it two arms.

    “Deliver one yellow source C cube into B_slot2, seated released stable, using B/C …”

    Controller to a new helper
    • 2arms handed over, B and C
    • 24robot actions by the helper
    • 7.8minutes until the arms come back
  3. 3

    Act

    One command, one arm

    Each move is one call to the robot tool. The call can ask for a fresh picture when the move ends.

    The agent sendsmove arm A to (−0.107, −0.973, 0.95) m at 0.12 m/s, then show camera A_front
    →
    The robot tool answers

    Completed, with a fresh picture from A_front

  4. 4

    Check

    Notice, and retry

    Later another helper grasps with arm C. The gripper closes at 0.065 m, not the 0.06 m expected. Nothing says what went wrong: the agent looks at the pictures and tries again.

    The agent sendsgrasp with arm C, expecting 0.06 m, then show C_front and C_side
    →
    The robot tool answers

    Grasp stopped outside expected object-width tolerance

The clock never stops. The hall runs in real time and does not wait while an agent thinks. A run ends when the agents say the job is done, or after 24 hours of work.

An evaluator they cannot reach. The agents run in a sandbox, away from the simulator and the scoring. When they say they are done, an evaluator checks the world itself.
